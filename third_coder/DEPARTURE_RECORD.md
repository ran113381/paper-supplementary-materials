# Departures from the deposited protocol: complete record

This file is the complete record of the departures from the protocol deposited before coding
(`protocol_third_coder_v2.7.md`, GitHub Release third-coder-protocol-v2.7, commit fd6616d).
Departures (a) to (e) below are departures (i) to (v) of Section 3.3 of the manuscript, in the same
order. References of the form "Section 5.3" are to the manuscript, "protocol Section" to the
deposited protocol, and Tables L1 and L2 to the response to reviewers. The text is that of the
response to reviewers.

All times are in a single local time zone; they are reported only to establish the sequence of events and the intervals between them. For each departure, the results that it can affect and those that it cannot are stated; the statements rest on the released outputs and logs.

(a) Order of sending, translation and declaration (protocol Section 11). On the first day, the administrator sent the English declaration at 10:26 and, at 12:41, one archive holding all materials; the materials therefore reached Coder 3 before a signed declaration was returned and before the push and Release, not after them. The archive held Chinese translations of the instructions and of the declaration, the codebook with an added Chinese translation column and a note that the English original prevails, the practice sheet and practice answer sheet with bilingual or Chinese column names, the coding sheet, byte-identical to the manifest version, and a note listing the files. Coder 3 returned signed copies of the English declaration at 15:20 and 15:36, in both of which the completion clause had also been signed with a time earlier than the sending of the coding sheet, and signed a new copy received at 16:31, on which the completion clause was again filled in before coding had finished; the SHA-256 of that copy is recorded in the protocol. No separate completion confirmation accompanied either the first returned sheet or the corrected sheet. Can affect: the labels, through the translation, whose effect cannot be separated from the third coder’s own judgement and is reported as a limitation (Section 5.3); without a completion confirmation, the returned sheets are the only record of completion. Cannot affect: which passages were coded, their order or their identifiers, because the coding sheet sent is byte-identical to the manifest version.

(b) Incomplete first sheet (protocol Sections 9.2, 10.3 and 10.5). The coding sheet was returned on the first day at 22:46:49 and hashed the next morning at 10:04, before any row was read, rather than on receipt (SHA-256 3d0aff4b1e55ee4eedc4a3f41ef52ece0e16e98e9aace52938377c9819306169); it labels every passage but lacks the lean for four unclear labels (sheet IDs A128, A218, A268 and A318), so under Section 10.3 it is incomplete and under Section 10.5 it should have been archived with no key opened against it. The analysis script reports this count without stopping. On the next day, its first stage opened the calibration key against this sheet and wrote the qualification record at 10:18:38 (outcome: qualified), so the authors knew that the third coder had qualified before the sheet was complete. Its second stage, whose log was written at 10:18:45, read the non-concordant and E1 keys and stopped at the first unclear label without a lean that the script encountered (sheet ID A268), before any comparison and without writing any label or metric. Can affect: every later step, including the request for the missing leans, was taken with the qualification outcome known. Cannot affect: the qualification statistics, which are identical in every reported value on the first sheet and on the corrected sheet; none of the four passages is a calibration passage.

(c) Changes after return (protocol Annex A, item 8). Annex A states that a returned sheet cannot be changed. After the first stage had run, the administrator, one of the authors, asked the third coder to complete the missing entries and communicated no result or passage identifier; the request is recorded, with its date and time, in the question-and-answer log released with the labels [QA-LOG: file name to be inserted when the log is added to `release/`]. The same author entered the third coder’s answers, on the author’s computer, into two successive copies of the sheet: one saved at 10:32:46 (SHA-256 4a624ddfb65f9c9916e0fe0e8637f439fdf3e5b2526bb46c6346c90ae020824f), which enters the four leans, each as 1, and one saved at 10:39:56 (SHA-256 e2602bd7d0f7737082c06c3fb1282eeddf43227153290aab7d2f8048ef9560bb), which in addition changes the confidence of A073 from High to Medium. Neither copy was returned by the third coder. The third coder then returned a corrected sheet, with the file time 11:45:16, which was hashed at 11:47:10, before any row was read (SHA-256 bfd18c9233c520098197f359fd194ea4a909e06bd3199d9669c672e6a8e4189f). Compared cell by cell without opening any key, the four files have the same rows, identifiers and passage texts and the same label for every passage. The corrected sheet differs from the first sheet in five cells: the lean of A128, A218, A268 and A318 is entered as 1, as in the author-entered copies, and the confidence of A074 is changed from High to Medium. The second author-entered copy therefore differs from the corrected sheet only in the confidence of A073 and A074, which are adjacent rows: the author entered the confidence change one row off, on A073 instead of A074. The file metadata of the corrected sheet record the same last editor as the first sheet, and a different one from the two author-entered copies. The analysis was first run, from the first stage, on the second author-entered copy and then rerun, from the first stage, on the corrected sheet; the results reported here are those of the rerun. The two runs produced byte-identical label files, counts, intervals and model-comparison logs and differ only in the hash of the qualification record, the sheet hash that it holds and its time of writing. The outputs of all three runs (the stopped run on the first sheet, the run on the author-entered copy and the rerun) are kept unchanged. Can affect: of the four added leans, the lean of A268, one of the seven unresolved passages, enters the lean sensitivity analysis and the calibration flip variant; the leans of A128, A218 and A318, three of the 27 unclear labels in the concordant near-miss layer, enter the reported leans of that layer (27 toward GenAI-related, none toward not GenAI-related). Cannot affect: the primary metrics, which exclude the seven unresolved passages; the bounds, which assign them 0 and 1 whatever the lean; the qualification statistics; the numbers of concordant non-matched passages that the third coder labeled GenAI-related or unclear, and their intervals; and any metric through a confidence rating, because confidence ratings enter no metric (A073 appears in no output, and A074 only in the list of unclear labels, which carries no confidence rating).

(d) Positive controls (protocol Section 8.1). Section 8.1 requires the analysis script to reproduce, before any new label is read, the previously published validation counts and metrics and the corresponding values on the 477 concordant passages. The analysis script holds no such control. The deposited model-comparison script holds a control for the previously published values; in all three runs it was run after the first and second stages had read the third coder’s labels, and in the two complete runs before the model comparison on the new labels. Can affect: the order of both safeguards. The values on the 477 concordant passages were computed only after the labels had been read, by the script that produced the note to Table L2 of the response to reviewers; that script asserts that the concordant labels are unchanged, which no re-adjudication alters, but holds no expected metric values stored before coding. Cannot affect: the reported values; the model-comparison control reads no label of the third coder, gave the same output in all three runs and passed, and it reproduces the previously published dictionary counts (216 true positives, 84 false positives, no false negatives and 300 true negatives).

(e) Implementation of the model comparison (protocol Sections 7.3, 8.2(i) and 8.3). The deposited model-comparison script accepts only a gold standard in which all 600 passages carry a binary label and computes no bootstrap interval, so the primary basis, which excludes the unresolved passages, cannot be computed with it. A wrapper script, written after the re-adjudicated labels were available, reproduces its computations; before use it reproduced, line by line, the deposited script’s log under the previous labels and its logs under the lean, the two bound and the flip-variant labels (five logs, all reproduced exactly). It then excludes the seven unresolved passages from every population and adds the F1 bootstrap: 2,000 resamples of passages within each population, percentile intervals, seed read from the deposited seed file whose SHA-256 is recorded in the protocol, with each method resampled separately, so the intervals are not paired across methods. For the rerun on the corrected sheet only its three input-path lines were changed, the five controls passed again, and its log is byte-identical to that of the earlier run. Can affect: the primary basis and the F1 intervals are computed by a script that was not deposited before coding. Cannot affect: any label, and any value that the deposited script itself computes, which the wrapper reproduces exactly. The wrapper, its logs and the deposited scripts are released in `third_coder/scripts/` (wrapper: `J21_q7_primary.py` for the first run and `J23_q7_primary.py` for the rerun, with `J23_vs_J21_script.diff`; deposited scripts: `analysis_third_coder.py`, `q7_construct_rescore.py`, `selection_third_coder.py`) and `third_coder/logs/`. A second script, released with the wrapper (`scripts/J29_run_r1_r4.py`; log `logs/J29_run_r1_r4.log`), reuses its definitions and the same seed file to compute the values on the 477 concordant passages, by stratum and under the previous labels (notes to Tables L1 and L2 of the response to reviewers).

## Summary

Departures (b) to (d) leave the primary metrics, the bounds and the qualification unchanged; departure (e) means that the primary metrics and the F1 intervals come from a script that was not deposited before coding, although it reproduces the deposited script exactly; and the effect of the translation on the labels cannot be separated from the third coder's own judgement. Together they mean that parts of the procedure were not carried out as the protocol set, in order or in form.
